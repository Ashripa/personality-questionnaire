## Scoring

- Raw scores converted to Uniform T-scores (M = 50, SD = 10) for clinical and content scales.
- Clinical significance threshold: T >= 65.
- Code types (two-point, three-point) form the basis of classical interpretation.

---

## Psychometric Properties

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Internal Consistency (Clinical Scales) | .34 -- .87 (median ~ .70) |
| Test-Retest (1 week, Clinical Scales) | .67 -- .92 |
| Normative Sample | 2,600 adults (1,138 males, 1,462 females) from 7 U.S. states |
| Cross-cultural translations | 20+ languages |

- Extensive empirical validity established through decades of research.
- Code-type interpretation supported by large clinical and forensic literatures.

## Chinese Version

- **Developers**: Fanny M. Cheung (CUHK), Weizhen Song (Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences), & Jianxin Zhang.
- **Normative Sample**: 1,553 males and 1,516 females from 7 major regions of China.
- **Publisher**: The Chinese University Press & Institute of Psychology, Academia Sinica (with permission from University of Minnesota Press).
- **Validation**: Translation equivalence confirmed through test-retest analyses, item endorsement pattern comparisons, and item desirability ratings across Hong Kong and mainland samples.
- **Song Weizhen** (1981) first adapted the MMPI for mainland China; Song's norms were widely used in Chinese clinical and research settings prior to the MMPI-2 standardization.
- **Reference**: Cheung, F. M., Song, W. Z., & Zhang, J. X. (2003). *The Chinese MMPI-2: Research and applications in clinical and nonclinical populations*. In J. N. Butcher (Ed.), *International adaptations of the MMPI-2*. University of Minnesota Press.
