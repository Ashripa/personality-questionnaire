# BIS/BAS Scales: Behavioral Inhibition System / Behavioral Activation System (24 Items, 20 Scored)

## Citation

Carver, C. S., & White, T. L. (1994). Behavioral inhibition, behavioral activation,
and affective responses to impending reward and punishment: The BIS/BAS Scales.
*Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 67*(2), 319-333.
https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.2.319

Official scale page: https://www.psy.miami.edu/faculty/ccarver/bisbas.html

---

## Overview

The BIS/BAS Scales measure individual differences in the sensitivity of two
motivational systems proposed by Gray's Reinforcement Sensitivity Theory:

- **Behavioral Inhibition System (BIS):** Regulates aversive motivation -- sensitivity
  to punishment, threat, and novel stimuli. Associated with anxiety and withdrawal.
- **Behavioral Activation System (BAS):** Regulates appetitive motivation -- sensitivity
  to reward, goal pursuit, and approach behavior. Contains three subfactors.

The questionnaire has 24 items total: 20 scored items across 4 subscales plus 4 filler
items that are not scored.

---

## Instructions to Respondent

> Each item of this questionnaire is a statement that a person may either agree with or
> disagree with. For each item, indicate how much you agree or disagree with what the
> item says. Please respond to all the items; do not leave any blank. Choose only one
> response to each statement. Please be as accurate and honest as you can be. Respond to
> each item as if it were the only item. That is, don't worry about being "consistent"
> in your responses.

## Scoring Key

### Step 1: Reverse-Score

All items EXCEPT items 2 and 22 need to be reverse-scored before summing.

Reverse scoring formula (for a 1-4 scale): Reversed = 5 - Original

| Original | Reversed |
|----------|----------|
| 1 | 4 |
| 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 1 |

Items 2 and 22 are scored as-is (they are worded in the low-BIS direction:
"I rarely experience fear" and "I have very few fears").

### Step 2: Sum Subscale Scores

After reverse-scoring, sum the items within each subscale:

| Subscale | Items | # Items | Score Range |
|----------|-------|---------|-------------|
| BIS | 2, 8, 13, 16, 19, 22, 24 | 7 | 7-28 |
| BAS-Drive | 3, 9, 12, 21 | 4 | 4-16 |
| BAS-Fun Seeking | 5, 10, 15, 20 | 4 | 4-16 |
| BAS-Reward Responsiveness | 4, 7, 14, 18, 23 | 5 | 5-20 |
| BAS Total | all 13 BAS items | 13 | 13-52 |

Higher scores indicate greater sensitivity of the respective system.

  https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01027

### Notes on Chinese Version

- The Chinese version by Li et al. (2008) uses a 4-point scale:
  1 = 完全同意, 2 = 有些同意, 3 = 有些不同意, 4 = 完全不同意
- Some Chinese adaptations removed filler items 1 and 18 (from the original numbering)
  due to low discriminative power, resulting in an 18-item version.
- A five-factor model (splitting BIS into BIS-Fear and BIS-Anxiety) has been found to
  fit well in Chinese populations.

## Theoretical Background

The BIS/BAS Scales are based on Jeffrey Gray's Reinforcement Sensitivity Theory (RST),
which proposes two fundamental neurobiological systems that regulate behavior:

1. **BIS (Behavioral Inhibition System):** Activated by signals of punishment,
   non-reward, novelty, and innate fear stimuli. Produces anxiety, inhibits ongoing
   behavior, and increases arousal and attention. Associated with the septo-hippocampal
   system.

2. **BAS (Behavioral Activation System):** Activated by signals of reward,
   non-punishment, and escape from punishment. Produces approach behavior, active
   avoidance, and positive affect. Associated with dopaminergic pathways.

### Clinical Relevance

- High BIS sensitivity is associated with anxiety disorders, depression, and
  neuroticism.
- High BAS sensitivity (especially BAS-Fun Seeking) is associated with impulsivity,
  substance use, and externalizing behavior.
- The BIS/BAS balance is relevant to understanding mood disorders, addiction, and
  personality traits.
