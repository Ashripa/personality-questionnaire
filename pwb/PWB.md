# PWB - Psychological Well-Being Scales

## Overview

The Psychological Well-Being Scales (PWB/PWBS) were developed by Carol D. Ryff (1989) to measure eudaimonic well-being based on a multidimensional model of psychological functioning. The scales operationalize six theoretically grounded dimensions of well-being derived from developmental, clinical, and humanistic psychology.

---

## Citation

- **Original:** Ryff, C. D. (1989). Happiness is everything, or is it? Explorations on the meaning of psychological well-being. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 57*(6), 1069-1081. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.57.6.1069
- **Factor structure:** Ryff, C. D., & Keyes, C. L. M. (1995). The structure of psychological well-being revisited. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 69*(4), 719-727.
- **Short forms:** Ryff, C. D., & Singer, B. H. (2006). Best news yet on the six-factor model of well-being. *Social Science Research, 35*(4), 1103-1119.
- **Chinese version:** Li, R. H. (2014). Reliability and validity of a shorter Chinese version for Ryff's Psychological Well-Being Scale. *Health Education Journal, 73*(4), 446-452.

## Scale Versions

| Version | Items per Dimension | Total Items |
|---------|:-------------------:|:-----------:|
| Full version | 14 | 84 |
| Medium | 9 | 54 |
| Short | 7 | 42 |
| Brief (most widely published) | 3 | 18 |

All versions measure the same six dimensions. The 18-item version is the most widely used short form and is documented in detail below.

---

## Six Dimensions

| Dimension | Definition |
|-----------|------------|
| **Autonomy** | Self-determination and independence; ability to resist social pressures; regulation of behavior from within |
| **Environmental Mastery** | Competence in managing the environment; effective use of surrounding opportunities; ability to create contexts suitable to personal needs |
| **Personal Growth** | Feelings of continued development; openness to new experiences; sense of realizing one's potential |
| **Positive Relations with Others** | Warm, trusting interpersonal relationships; concern for the welfare of others; capacity for empathy and intimacy |
| **Purpose in Life** | Sense of directedness and intentionality; goals and objectives for living; feeling that life has meaning |
| **Self-Acceptance** | Positive attitude toward the self; acknowledgment of multiple aspects of self, including good and bad qualities |

## Instructions (18-Item Version)

Please indicate your degree of agreement with each statement using the following scale:

| Score | Label |
|:-----:|-------|

## Scoring

### Reverse Scoring

For items marked "R" (items 4, 5, 6, 7, 10, 14, 15, 16), reverse the score:

| Original | Reversed |
|:--------:|:--------:|
| 1 | 6 |
| 2 | 5 |
| 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 3 |
| 5 | 2 |
| 6 | 1 |

### Computing Scores

- **Subscale scores:** Sum the three items within each dimension after reverse scoring. Range: 3-18 per subscale.
- **Total score:** Sum all 18 items after reverse scoring. Range: 18-108.
- Higher scores indicate greater psychological well-being.

---

## Psychometric Properties (18-Item Version)

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Internal consistency (per subscale) | Cronbach's alpha = 0.33-0.56 (lower due to only 3 items per subscale) |
| Internal consistency (total) | Cronbach's alpha = 0.70-0.84 |
| Factor structure | Six correlated factors (confirmed via CFA) |
| Convergent validity | Correlates with life satisfaction, positive affect, self-esteem |
| Discriminant validity | Negatively correlates with depression, anxiety, negative affect |

**Note:** The 18-item version has lower subscale reliability than longer versions due to having only 3 items per dimension. Using the total score or longer versions is recommended when subscale-level reliability is important.

## Full 42-Item Version Structure (7 Items per Dimension)

The 42-item version uses the same six dimensions, with 7 items each. Each dimension includes a mix of positively worded and reverse-scored items. The following lists the 42-item version for reference.

### Autonomy (7 items)

| Item | Scoring |
|------|:-------:|
| I have confidence in my opinions, even if they are contrary to the general consensus. | + |
| I judge myself by what I think is important, not by the values of what others think is important. | + |
| I am not afraid to voice my opinions, even when they are in opposition to the opinions of most people. | + |
| My decisions are not usually influenced by what everyone else is doing. | + |
| I tend to be influenced by people with strong opinions. | R |
| I tend to worry about what other people think of me. | R |
| It's difficult for me to voice my own opinions on controversial matters. | R |

### Environmental Mastery (7 items)

| Item | Scoring |
|------|:-------:|
| In general, I feel I am in charge of the situation in which I live. | + |
| I am quite good at managing the many responsibilities of my daily life. | + |
| I have been able to build a home and a lifestyle for myself that is much to my liking. | + |
| The demands of everyday life often get me down. | R |
| I often feel overwhelmed by my responsibilities. | R |
| I do not fit very well with the people and the community around me. | R |
| I have difficulty arranging my life in a way that is satisfying to me. | R |

### Personal Growth (7 items)

| Item | Scoring |
|------|:-------:|
| I think it is important to have new experiences that challenge how you think about yourself and the world. | + |
| For me, life has been a continuous process of learning, changing and growth. | + |
| I have a sense that I have developed a lot as a person over time. | + |
| I am not interested in activities that will expand my horizons. | R |
| I gave up trying to make big improvements or changes in my life a long time ago. | R |
| I do not enjoy being in new situations that require me to change my old familiar ways of doing things. | R |
| When I think about it, I haven't really improved much as a person over the years. | R |

### Positive Relations with Others (7 items)

| Item | Scoring |
|------|:-------:|
| People would describe me as a giving person, willing to share my time with others. | + |
| I enjoy personal and mutual conversations with family members or friends. | + |
| Most people see me as loving and affectionate. | + |
| I know that I can trust my friends, and they know they can trust me. | + |
| Maintaining close relationships has been difficult and frustrating for me. | R |
| I often feel lonely because I have few close friends with whom to share my concerns. | R |
| I have not experienced many warm and trusting relationships with others. | R |

### Purpose in Life (7 items)

| Item | Scoring |
|------|:-------:|
| Some people wander aimlessly through life, but I am not one of them. | + |
| I enjoy making plans for the future and working to make them a reality. | + |
| I have a sense of direction and purpose in life. | + |
| I live life one day at a time and don't really think about the future. | R |
| My daily activities often seem trivial and unimportant to me. | R |
| I don't have a good sense of what it is I'm trying to accomplish in life. | R |
| I sometimes feel as if I've done all there is to do in life. | R |

### Self-Acceptance (7 items)

| Item | Scoring |
|------|:-------:|
| When I look at the story of my life, I am pleased with how things have turned out. | + |
| In general, I feel confident and positive about myself. | + |
| I like most aspects of my personality. | + |
| When I compare myself to friends and acquaintances, it makes me feel good about who I am. | + |
| I feel like many of the people I know have gotten more out of life than I have. | R |
| In many ways, I feel disappointed about my achievements in life. | R |
| My attitude about myself is probably not as positive as most people feel about themselves. | R |

## Chinese Version Notes

Several Chinese translations and validations of the PWB scales have been conducted:

- **84-item version:** Adapted and validated in Chinese populations with the standard 6-point Likert scale.
- **18-item version:** Li (2014) developed and validated a shorter Chinese version demonstrating acceptable psychometric properties.
- The six-factor structure has generally been replicated in Chinese samples, though some studies report better fit with a higher-order general well-being factor.

Researchers using the Chinese version should cite the relevant validation study and obtain the validated Chinese translation from the original authors.
