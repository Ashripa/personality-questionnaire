## Overview

The VIA Inventory of Strengths (VIA-IS) is a self-report questionnaire developed by Christopher Peterson and Martin Seligman to measure 24 character strengths grouped under 6 broad virtues. It is the primary assessment tool of the Values in Action (VIA) Classification, which serves as the positive psychology counterpart to the DSM -- classifying positive human traits rather than deficits. The classification was developed by a team of 55 scientists who systematically reviewed psychological, philosophical, and theological literature to identify universally valued positive traits across cultures.

## The 6 Virtues and 24 Character Strengths

### I. Wisdom (Cognitive strengths that entail the acquisition and use of knowledge)

| # | Strength | Definition |
|---|----------|------------|
| 1 | **Creativity** | Conceptualizing something useful; coming up with ideas that result in something worthwhile. Thinking of novel and productive ways to do things. |
| 2 | **Curiosity** | Seeking out situations where one gains new experiences; taking an interest in ongoing experience for its own sake; exploring and discovering. |
| 3 | **Judgment** (Open-mindedness) | Weighing all aspects objectively in making decisions, including arguments that conflict with one's own convictions; thinking things through and examining them from all sides. |
| 4 | **Love of Learning** | Being motivated to acquire new levels of knowledge, or to deepen existing knowledge or skills; mastering new topics and bodies of knowledge. |
| 5 | **Perspective** (Wisdom) | Giving advice to others by considering different and relevant perspectives; using one's own experiences and knowledge to make sense of the world. |

### II. Courage (Emotional strengths that involve the exercise of will to accomplish goals in the face of opposition)

| # | Strength | Definition |
|---|----------|------------|
| 6 | **Bravery** | Acting on convictions and facing threats, challenges, difficulties, and pains, despite doubts and fears; not shrinking from threat or difficulty. |
| 7 | **Honesty** (Authenticity, Integrity) | Being honest to oneself and to others; presenting oneself and one's reactions accurately to each person; taking responsibility for one's feelings and actions. |
| 8 | **Perseverance** (Industriousness) | Persisting toward goals despite obstacles, discouragements, or disappointments; finishing what one starts. |
| 9 | **Zest** (Vitality, Enthusiasm) | Feeling vital and full of energy; approaching life feeling activated and enthusiastic; living life as an adventure. |

### III. Humanity (Interpersonal strengths that involve tending and befriending others)

| # | Strength | Definition |
|---|----------|------------|
| 10 | **Kindness** (Generosity) | Being helpful and empathic; regularly doing nice favors for others without expecting anything in return. |
| 11 | **Love** | Experiencing close, loving relationships characterized by giving and receiving love, warmth, and caring. |
| 12 | **Social Intelligence** | Being aware of and understanding one's own feelings and thoughts, as well as the feelings of those around one; knowing what makes other people tick. |

### IV. Justice (Civic strengths that underlie healthy community life)

| # | Strength | Definition |
|---|----------|------------|
| 13 | **Fairness** | Treating everyone equally and fairly; giving everyone the same chance; applying the same rules to everyone; not letting personal feelings bias decisions about others. |
| 14 | **Leadership** | Taking charge and guiding groups to meaningful goals; ensuring good relations among group members; organizing group activities. |
| 15 | **Teamwork** (Citizenship) | Being a helpful and contributing group and team member; feeling responsible for helping the team reach its goals; working well as a member of a group. |

### V. Temperance (Strengths that protect against excess)

| # | Strength | Definition |
|---|----------|------------|
| 16 | **Forgiveness** (Mercy) | Forgiving others when they have done wrong; accepting others' shortcomings; giving people a second chance; not being vengeful. |
| 17 | **Humility** (Modesty) | Seeing one's strengths and talents without seeking to be the center of attention or to receive recognition; letting accomplishments speak for themselves. |
| 18 | **Prudence** | Acting carefully and cautiously; looking to avoid unnecessary risks; planning with the future in mind; not saying or doing things that might later be regretted. |
| 19 | **Self-Regulation** (Self-control) | Managing one's feelings and actions; being disciplined and self-controlled; regulating what one feels and does. |

### VI. Transcendence (Strengths that forge connections to the larger universe and provide meaning)

| # | Strength | Definition |
|---|----------|------------|
| 20 | **Appreciation of Beauty & Excellence** | Recognizing, emotionally experiencing, and appreciating beauty around oneself and the skill of others in various domains of life. |
| 21 | **Gratitude** | Being grateful for many things; expressing thankfulness to others; being aware of and thankful for the good things that happen. |
| 22 | **Hope** (Optimism) | Being realistic and also full of optimism about the future; believing in one's actions and feeling confident things will turn out well; expecting the best. |
| 23 | **Humor** (Playfulness) | Approaching life playfully; making others laugh; finding humor in difficult and stressful times; liking to laugh and tease. |
| 24 | **Spirituality** (Religiousness, Faith) | Feeling spiritual and believing in a sense of purpose or meaning in life; seeing one's place in the grand scheme of the universe; having coherent beliefs about the higher purpose and meaning of life. |

## Scale Format and Instructions

### Original VIA-IS (240 items)

- **Items:** 240 statements (10 items per character strength)
- **Response scale:** 5-point Likert scale
  - 1 = Very Much Unlike Me
  - 2 = Unlike Me
  - 3 = Neutral
  - 4 = Like Me
  - 5 = Very Much Like Me
- **Keying:** All items positively keyed
- **Completion time:** Approximately 30-40 minutes
- **Scoring:** Mean score for each strength's 10 items; strengths are ranked from highest to lowest. The top 5 are typically designated as "Signature Strengths."

### Instructions (general form)

Respondents are asked to rate how well each statement describes them. There is no right or wrong answer. The goal is an honest self-appraisal of one's typical behavior and experience.

### VIA-IS-R Psychometric Properties

- Cronbach's alpha: 0.77-0.91 (mean = 0.85) for 24 strength scales
- Virtue scale alpha: 0.76-0.83
- Scores are interchangeable across VIA-IS, VIA-IS-P, and VIA-IS-M versions

### Chinese Adaptations in Research

- **Chinese Virtues Questionnaire-96 (CVQ-96):** A culturally adapted version in simplified Chinese with 96 items (4 per strength). Developed after 144 items were removed from the original 240 due to low item-loading and lack of cultural validity.
- **Chinese VIA Youth-96:** Validated for use with Chinese adolescents; shows good fit for the original four-factor structure; character strength scores significantly correlated with life satisfaction and self-efficacy.

## Copyright Status

- **The VIA Classification (6 virtues, 24 strengths, and their definitions)** is publicly available and widely cited in academic literature.
- **The actual VIA-IS questionnaire items are proprietary** and owned by the VIA Institute on Character. The full 240-item / 192-item banks **are not publicly released**. They may not be reproduced, distributed, or used without permission.
- The survey is offered **free for individual use** at viacharacter.org, but this does not grant rights to reproduce items.
- **Researchers** must register with the VIA Institute to use the items in studies and must follow usage permissions.
- Items that appear in published journal articles may be **cited in limited form** (e.g., the sample items in Table A), but reproducing the full scale is not permitted without authorization.
- **The IPIP-VIA analog items (Table B) are in the public domain** and are exempt from the above restriction — they may be freely used.
