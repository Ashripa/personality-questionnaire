# FFMQ - Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire

## Citation

Baer, R. A., Smith, G. T., Hopkins, J., Krietemeyer, J., & Toney, L. (2006). Using self-report assessment methods to explore facets of mindfulness. *Assessment*, 13(1), 27-45.

Chinese version:
Deng, Y. Q., Liu, X. H., Rodriguez, M. A., & Xia, C. Y. (2011). The Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire: Psychometric properties of the Chinese version. *Mindfulness*, 2(2), 123-128.

**Copyright**: Available for research use. The FFMQ emerged from factor analysis of five existing mindfulness questionnaires (FMI, MAAS, KIMS, CAMS-R, MQ). Items available at ruthbaer.com/academics/.

---

## Instructions

> Please rate each of the following statements using the scale provided. Write the number in the blank that best describes your own opinion of what is generally true for you.

## Response Scale (5-point Likert)

| Score | English | 中文 |
|-------|---------|------|
| 1 | Never or very rarely true | 一点也不符合 |
| 2 | Rarely true | 较少符合 |
| 3 | Sometimes true | 有些符合 |
| 4 | Often true | 非常符合 |
| 5 | Very often or always true | 完全符合 |

### Psychometric Properties

- English: Cronbach's α = .75–.91 per facet
- Chinese (Deng et al., 2011): α = .45–.84 per facet; Non-Reactivity α lowest (.45)
- Five-factor CFA confirmed in both English and Chinese samples
