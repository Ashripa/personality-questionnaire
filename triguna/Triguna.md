# Triguna Personality Scales

**Priority:** P5
**Category:** Indigenous / culturally-derived personality assessment
**Status:** Research instruments (multiple versions)

---

## Overview

Triguna personality scales operationalize the ancient Indian (Vedic) concept of the three Gunas -- Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas -- as measurable personality dimensions. Multiple researchers have developed psychometric instruments to assess these qualities, which originate from Samkhya philosophy and are extensively described in texts such as the Bhagavad Gita and Ayurvedic medical treatises.

## Theoretical Foundation

### The Three Gunas

In Samkhya and Vedantic philosophy, all of material nature (Prakriti) is composed of three fundamental qualities (Gunas):

| Guna | Sanskrit Meaning | Psychological Qualities | Behavioral Tendencies |
|------|-----------------|------------------------|----------------------|
| **Sattva** | Purity, goodness | Clarity, wisdom, harmony, self-control | Selfless service, truthfulness, non-violence, detachment, knowledge-seeking |
| **Rajas** | Passion, activity | Ambition, desire, restlessness, attachment | Achievement-orientation, competitiveness, agitation, sensory pleasure |
| **Tamas** | Darkness, inertia | Ignorance, lethargy, delusion, rigidity | Laziness, negligence, procrastination, excessive sleep, resistance to change |

### Key Principles
- Every individual possesses all three Gunas in varying proportions
- The dominant Guna shapes personality and behavioral tendencies
- Gunas are dynamic and can shift through lifestyle, diet, spiritual practice, and environment
- Sattva is considered the ideal state; spiritual development involves increasing Sattva and reducing Rajas and Tamas
- The Gunas interact: Rajas can activate either Sattva or Tamas

## Major Instruments

### 1. Mysore Triguna Scale (MTS)
- **Developers:** Shilpa & Venkatesha Murthy (2012)
- **Items:** 63 items (original); 15 items (short form)
- **Traits identified:** 189 total (100 Sattva, 60 Rajas, 29 Tamas)
- **Format:** Multiple-choice
- **Reliability:** Cronbach's alpha: Sattva .969, Rajas .911, Tamas .876

### 2. Vedic Personality Inventory (VPI)
- **Developer:** Wolf (1998)
- **Items:** 56 items
- **Scales:** Sattva, Rajas, Tamas
- **Format:** 5-point Likert scale
- **Reliability:** Cronbach's alpha .90-.94

### 3. Guna Inventory
- **Developer:** Das (1991)
- **Items:** 30 items (10 per Guna)
- **Format:** 3-point rating scale

### 4. Trigunatmak Personality Inventory
- **Developers:** Sitamma, Sridevi, & Krishna Rao (1995)
- **Items:** 90 items (30 per Guna)
- **Publisher:** National Psychological Corporation, India

## Psychometric Properties (Aggregated Across Instruments)

### Reliability
- Internal consistency generally good to excellent (alpha .84-.97)
- Test-retest reliability adequate in reported studies

### Validity
- Content validity: Established through expert review and textual analysis of classical sources
- Convergent validity: Sattva correlates positively with Big Five Openness, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness; Tamas correlates with Neuroticism; Rajas shows mixed patterns
- Criterion validity: Sattva associated with life satisfaction, psychological well-being, and prosocial behavior; Tamas associated with perceived stress and psychopathology
- Discriminant validity between Gunas generally supported

### Limitations
- Most validation samples are from India, limiting cross-cultural generalizability
- Social desirability concerns: Sattva items are transparently positive, Tamas items transparently negative
- Classical Guna theory posits dynamic states, but instruments measure them as traits
- Items are derived from religious/philosophical texts, raising questions about secular applicability
- Limited independent replication outside Indian psychology community
- Translation and cultural equivalence issues for non-Indian populations

## Research Applications

- Relationship between Gunas and Big Five personality traits
- Gunas and mental health outcomes (well-being, stress, psychiatric conditions)
- Ayurvedic medicine and mind-body constitution (Prakriti)
- Yoga and meditation research
- Organizational behavior in Indian contexts
- Cross-cultural personality psychology

## Notes

- Triguna scales represent an important contribution to indigenous psychology, operationalizing concepts from one of the world's oldest personality theories
- They challenge Western-centric assumptions in personality psychology by deriving constructs from a non-Western philosophical tradition
- The dynamic, state-like quality of Gunas in classical theory versus their measurement as stable traits remains an unresolved theoretical tension
- Growing interest in integrating Triguna with modern personality frameworks (e.g., mapping onto Big Five or HEXACO)
