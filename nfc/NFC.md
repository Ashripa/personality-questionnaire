## Overview

The Need for Cognition Scale (NCS / NFC) measures individual differences in the intrinsic motivation to engage in and enjoy effortful cognitive activities. It captures the tendency to seek out, enjoy, and engage in thinking for its own sake, independent of external rewards. NFC is a motivational measure, not an ability measure -- it assesses what people are inclined to do cognitively rather than what they are capable of doing.

The NFC is closely tied to the **Elaboration Likelihood Model** of persuasion (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986). High NFC individuals are more likely to engage in central route processing, while low NFC individuals rely more on heuristics and peripheral cues.

## Instructions

English:
> For each of the statements below, please indicate whether or not the statement is characteristic of you or of what you believe. For example, if the statement is extremely uncharacteristic of you or of what you believe about yourself (not at all like you) please place a "1" on the line to the left of the statement. If the statement is extremely characteristic of you or of what you believe about yourself (very much like you) please place a "5" on the line to the left of the statement.

中文指导语：
> 以下由心理学家们所拟写的问卷，请根据自己的情况，选择最符合的选项。

| Score | English | 中文 |
|-------|---------|------|

| 2 | Somewhat uncharacteristic of me | 有些不符合 |
| 3 | Uncertain | 不确定 |
| 4 | Somewhat characteristic of me | 有些符合 |

Note: The original 34-item scale (Cacioppo & Petty, 1982, Study 1) used a 9-point agreement format ranging from -4 (very strong disagreement) to +4 (very strong agreement). The 5-point format shown above is the standard for the 18-item short form.

## Scoring

### Forward-scored items (9 items)
Items 1, 2, 6, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 18

### Reverse-scored items (9 items)
Items 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 16, 17

### Scoring Procedure

1. **Reverse score** the 9 designated items by subtracting the response from 6:
   - 1 -> 5, 2 -> 4, 3 -> 3, 4 -> 2, 5 -> 1
2. **Sum all 18 item responses** (after reverse scoring)
   - Score range: 18-90
3. **Optionally compute a mean score** by dividing the total by 18
   - Mean score range: 1.00-5.00
4. **Higher scores** = greater need for cognition (stronger intrinsic motivation to engage in effortful thinking)

### Structure

- **Unidimensional** -- no subscales; a single dominant factor
- The single factor accounts for approximately 37% of variance in the 18-item form (Cacioppo et al., 1984)

### Interpretation

No published cutoff scores or normative interpretation bands exist. The scale is used as a continuous individual-difference measure. Some approximate reference data:

| Sample | N | Mean | SD | Source |
|--------|---|------|----|--------|
| US Freshmen | 93 | 61.4 | 12.5 | MUW (2013) |
| US Freshmen | 108 | 60.6 | 9.8 | MUW (2015) |
| US Seniors | 59 | 66.4 | 9.9 | MUW (2014) |
| US Seniors | 48 | 68.4 | 9.2 | MUW (2015) |

- **High scorers**: Enjoy cognitive challenges, process information systematically, resistant to superficial persuasion, more likely to differentiate strong from weak arguments
- **Low scorers**: Prefer simple tasks, rely more on heuristics and peripheral cues, avoid unnecessary cognitive effort
- **Gender differences**: No significant differences typically found (Cacioppo & Petty, 1982)
- **Age effects**: Small negative association (meta-analytic r ~ -.17; Cacioppo et al., 1996)

## Psychometric Properties

### Reliability

| Property | Value | Source |
|----------|-------|--------|
| Internal consistency (alpha) -- NFC-18 | .90 (theta/maximized alpha) | Cacioppo, Petty & Kao (1984) |
| Internal consistency (alpha) -- NFC-34 | .91 | Cacioppo, Petty & Kao (1984) |
| Internal consistency (alpha) -- US general population | .94 | Lins de Holanda Coelho et al. (2020) |
| Internal consistency (alpha) -- UK general population | .91 | Lins de Holanda Coelho et al. (2020) |
| Split-half reliability -- NFC-34 | .87 (Spearman-Brown) | Cacioppo & Petty (1982) |
| Cross-version correlation (NFC-18 vs. NFC-34) | r = .95 | Cacioppo, Petty & Kao (1984) |
| Test-retest reliability | adequate (reported stable) | Sadowski & Gulgoz (1992); Verplanken (1991) |

### Validity

**Factor structure:**
- Unidimensional: single dominant factor (Cacioppo & Petty, 1982; Cacioppo et al., 1984)
- CFA: CFI = .89-.92 for NFC-18 (Lins de Holanda Coelho et al., 2020)

**Convergent validity:**
- Openness to Experience (Big Five): r = .41; Openness to Ideas facet: r = .67 (Fleischhauer et al., 2010)
- Conscientiousness: r = .25; Persistence: r = .30 (Fleischhauer et al., 2010)
- Individual-oriented achievement motivation: moderate positive correlation (高泉豐, 1994)

**Discriminant validity:**
- Intelligence (fluid): r ~ .25; ACT scores: r = .39 -- related but distinct from cognitive ability
- Neuroticism: r = -.26 (Fleischhauer et al., 2010)
- Dogmatism: r = -.27 (Cacioppo & Petty, 1982)
- Social desirability: r = .08 (ns, anonymous); r = .21 (non-anonymous) (Cacioppo & Petty, 1982)
- Test anxiety, locus of control, social skills: weak correlations (高泉豐, 1994)

**Behavioral correlates:**
- Higher NFC individuals spend more time reading and engage more in intellectual leisure activities
- Higher NFC individuals spend less time watching television
- Higher NFC is associated with stronger central route processing of persuasive messages
- Higher NFC is associated with better argument scrutiny and resistance to peripheral persuasion cues

### Chinese Version Psychometric Properties

**Taiwan version (高泉豐, 1994):**
- Adequate internal consistency and test-retest reliability
- Single dominant factor confirmed by factor analysis
- Convergent and discriminant validity supported
- Sample: 1195 high school and college students in northern Taiwan

**Mainland China version (邝怡 et al., 2005):**
- 17 items (one item removed from original 18 during adaptation)
- Validated with Chinese university students

## Chinese Version Details (中文版说明)

### Translation History

The earliest and most authoritative Chinese version was developed by **高泉豐** (Kao, Chuan Feng) in 1994 -- notably the same Kao who co-authored the English NFC-18 with Cacioppo and Petty in 1984. Published in the *Chinese Journal of Psychology* (中華心理學刊), this Taiwan-based adaptation used the standard translation-back-translation procedure and was validated with 1195 high school and college students.

For mainland China, **邝怡, 朱莉琪, 刘力 (2005)** published a revised Chinese version with 17 items, adapted for Chinese university students. Various other mainland China researchers have since used Chinese translations of the NFC in their studies, often as a criterion measure when validating related scales (e.g., 刘雪峰 et al., 2007 used the NFC to establish criterion-related validity of the Chinese Need for Closure Scale).

The simplified Chinese version presented in the items table above is the version widely circulated in mainland China research contexts. Users conducting formal research should consult 高泉豐 (1994) or 邝怡 et al. (2005) for the published validated versions and cite accordingly.
