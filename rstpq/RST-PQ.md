# Reinforcement Sensitivity Theory of Personality Questionnaire (RST-PQ)

## Citation

Corr, P. J., & Cooper, A. J. (2016). The Reinforcement Sensitivity Theory of Personality Questionnaire (RST-PQ): Development and validation. *Psychological Assessment, 28*(11), 1427-1440. https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0000273

## Overview

The RST-PQ is a 65-item self-report questionnaire designed to measure personality traits according to the revised Reinforcement Sensitivity Theory (rRST; Gray & McNaughton, 2000). It assesses three neuropsychological systems across 6 subscales:

- **Behavioral Approach System (BAS)** -- 4 subscales:
  - Reward Interest (RI) -- 7 items
  - Goal-Drive Persistence (GDP) -- 7 items
  - Reward Reactivity (RR) -- 10 items
  - Impulsivity (Imp) -- 8 items
- **Fight-Flight-Freeze System (FFFS)** -- 1 subscale: 10 items
- **Behavioral Inhibition System (BIS)** -- 1 subscale: 23 items

**Total: 65 items**

Items were developed to avoid ambiguity with emotional words, drawing from existing RST instruments, qualitative responses to defensive and approach situations, and theoretical concepts of the revised RST.

## Instructions

> Please answer the following questions by selecting the response that best describes you. There are no right or wrong answers. Please answer every statement, even if you are not entirely sure of the answer.

### Scoring Method

1. **No reverse-scored items.** All items are scored in the keyed direction (1-4). Higher scores indicate greater sensitivity on that dimension.
2. **Subscale score** = sum of all items in that subscale.
3. **Score ranges:**

| Subscale | Min | Max |
|----------|-----|-----|
| BIS | 23 | 92 |
| FFFS | 10 | 40 |
| BAS-RI | 7 | 28 |
| BAS-GDP | 7 | 28 |
| BAS-RR | 10 | 40 |
| BAS-Imp | 8 | 32 |

**Note on Item 22:** Some secondary sources have labeled item 22 ("I think I should 'stop and think' more instead of jumping into things too quickly") as potentially requiring reverse scoring. However, in the original Corr & Cooper (2016) publication, all items are scored in the forward direction. Agreeing with this statement reflects recognition of one's own impulsivity, and the item loads positively on the Impulsivity factor. Researchers should consult the original publication and supplemental materials for definitive scoring guidance.

### Items Not Belonging to Any Subscale

The following item numbers do not appear in any of the 6 subscales. They may be filler items or belong to an experimental Defensive Fight (DF) subscale that was explored but not included in the standard 65-item scoring: none -- all 65 items are assigned to the 6 subscales listed above.

**Note on the Defensive Fight (DF) scale:** Corr & Cooper (2016) also developed an 8-item Defensive Fight subscale (listed in their Appendix D), which extends the questionnaire to 73 items. This DF scale is considered supplementary and is not part of the standard 65-item RST-PQ.

## Chinese Version

As of 2026, no formally validated Chinese (Mandarin) translation of the RST-PQ has been identified in the published literature. Validated translations exist for several languages including Spanish, Swedish, German, Persian, Dutch, and Arabic, but a Chinese version has not been published in major English-language or Chinese-language databases.

Researchers wishing to use the RST-PQ in Chinese-speaking populations should:
1. Contact the original authors (Philip J. Corr and Andrew J. Cooper) for permission.
2. Follow standard translation-back-translation procedures (e.g., Brislin, 1970).
3. Conduct a local validation study.

### Factor Structure

Corr & Cooper (2016) confirmed the 6-factor structure through both exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA):
- The 6-factor solution showed good fit across multiple samples.
- BIS and FFFS emerged as clearly separable defensive systems, consistent with rRST predictions.
- The 4-factor BAS structure (RI, GDP, RR, Imp) was supported, demonstrating that approach motivation is multidimensional.

### Convergent and Discriminant Validity

From Corr & Cooper (2016):
- BIS correlated positively with Neuroticism (EPQ-R) and negatively with Extraversion.
- BAS subscales (especially RI and RR) correlated positively with Extraversion.
- FFFS correlated positively with Neuroticism but was distinguishable from BIS.
- BAS-Imp correlated with Psychoticism (EPQ-R).
- The RST-PQ showed expected patterns of convergent validity with Carver & White's BIS/BAS Scales while providing greater differentiation of approach subsystems.
