## Scoring

- Raw scores are summed per facet (8 items) and per domain (48 items).
- Items are balanced (approximately half reverse-scored) to control for acquiescence.
- Raw scores are converted to T-scores (M = 50, SD = 10) using age- and sex-specific norms.
- Interpretive ranges: Very Low (< 35), Low (35--44), Average (45--55), High (56--65), Very High (> 65).

| Property | Domain Scales | Facet Scales |
|---|---|---|
| Internal Consistency (alpha) | .86 -- .95 | .56 -- .90 |
| Test-Retest (6 years) | .63 -- .83 | -- |
| Cross-observer agreement (Form S vs R) | .45 -- .60 | -- |

- Factor structure replicable across cultures, age groups, and languages.
- Convergent and discriminant validity established with other personality instruments (e.g., MBTI, 16PF, MMPI).
- Validated against projective techniques and peer ratings.

## Chinese Version

- **Translation**: Translated into Chinese by Michael Bond and associates (Michelle Yik, Eddie Ho, F. Y. Liu, Kwok Leung); used in Hong Kong Chinese student samples.
- **Mainland China**: Yang Jian et al. adapted the NEO-PI-R for mainland Chinese populations. A revised Chinese NEO-PI-3 version has also been developed.
- **Reliability**: Chinese version shows internal consistency comparable to the English version; the six-facet model achieves satisfactory fit with measurement equivalence across gender.
- **Validity**: Factor structure replicated in Chinese samples; joint factor analysis with CPAI reveals four shared factors plus two culture-specific factors.
- **Reference**: McCrae, R. R., Costa, P. T., Jr., & Yik, M. S. M. (1996). Universal aspects of Chinese personality structure. In M. H. Bond (Ed.), *The handbook of Chinese psychology* (pp. 189--207). Oxford University Press.
