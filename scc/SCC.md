# SCC: Self-Concept Clarity Scale

## Citation

Campbell, J. D., Trapnell, P. D., Heine, S. J., Katz, I. M., Lavallee, L. F., & Lehman, D. R. (1996). Self-concept clarity: Measurement, personality correlates, and cultural boundaries. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, *70*(1), 141-156. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.70.1.141

Chinese adaptation:

Campbell, J. D. et al. (1996) scale translated and validated in Chinese by multiple research groups, including 牛更枫 et al. (2016) (α = .81-.83).

## Overview

The Self-Concept Clarity Scale (SCCS / SCC) is a 12-item self-report measure assessing **self-concept clarity** -- the extent to which the contents of an individual's self-concept (i.e., perceived personal attributes) are clearly and confidently defined, internally consistent, and temporally stable. Low self-concept clarity is associated with low self-esteem, high neuroticism, chronic self-analysis, and ruminative self-focus.

## Instructions

> For each of the following statements, please indicate the degree to which it is characteristic of you by circling the appropriate number. A response of 1 means that you strongly disagree with the statement, and a response of 5 means that you strongly agree.

## Response Scale

| Score | Label |
|-------|-------|
| 1 | Strongly disagree |
| 2 | Disagree |
| 3 | Neither agree nor disagree |
| 4 | Agree |
| 5 | Strongly agree |

## Reverse Scoring

Items **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12** are reverse-scored (10 items total). For these items, recode as follows:

| Original | Recoded |
|----------|---------|
| 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 4 |
| 3 | 3 |
| 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 1 |

Items **6** and **11** are NOT reverse-scored (they are positively worded items indicating clarity).

## Scoring Method

1. Reverse-score items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 12.
2. Sum all 12 items to obtain a **total SCC score**.
3. Score range: **12--60**.
4. Higher scores indicate **greater** self-concept clarity.
5. There are no subscales; the SCC is a unidimensional measure.
6. Mean scores in college samples typically range from approximately 36 to 42.

## Psychometric Properties

### Original Validation (Campbell et al., 1996)

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Cronbach's alpha | 0.86 |
| 4-month test-retest *r* | 0.79 |
| 5-month test-retest *r* | 0.70 |

- Exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses support a single-factor structure.
- Convergent validity: SCC correlates significantly with self-esteem (*r* = .61), internal locus of control (*r* = .35), and inversely with neuroticism (*r* = -.63), chronic self-analysis (*r* = -.47), and ruminative self-focus (*r* = -.49).
- Discriminant validity: SCC is distinct from self-esteem level, demonstrating unique predictive power for psychological adjustment outcomes.
- Cultural note: North American samples show higher SCC than Japanese samples, suggesting cultural boundaries in the construct.

### Chinese Version

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Cronbach's alpha | 0.83 |

- The two-factor structure (self-confidence level and stability-consistency) has been reported in some Chinese validation studies, though the unidimensional structure is also supported.
- The Chinese version has been validated with both college students and middle-school students.
