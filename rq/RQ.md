## Overview

The Relationship Questionnaire (RQ) is a single-item (4-paragraph) self-report measure of adult attachment style, based on Bartholomew's (1990) four-category model of attachment. The model crosses two dimensions -- **Model of Self** (positive vs. negative) and **Model of Other** (positive vs. negative) -- to yield four attachment prototypes:

| | Positive Model of Other | Negative Model of Other |
|:---|:---:|:---:|
| **Positive Model of Self** | Secure | Dismissing |
| **Negative Model of Self** | Preoccupied | Fearful |

The RQ extends Hazan and Shaver's (1987) original three-category measure by splitting the avoidant category into dismissing-avoidant and fearful-avoidant.

---

## Instructions

### Part 1: Forced Choice

> Following are four general relationship styles that people often report. Please read each description and **circle the letter corresponding to the style that best describes you** or is closest to the way you generally are in your close relationships.

### Part 2: Continuous Ratings

> Now please rate each of the relationship styles above to indicate **how well or poorly each description corresponds to your general relationship style**.

## Items (Four Attachment Prototype Descriptions)

### Style A -- Secure

> It is easy for me to become emotionally close to others. I am comfortable depending on them and having them depend on me. I don't worry about being alone or having others not accept me.

### Style B -- Fearful

> I am uncomfortable getting close to others. I want emotionally close relationships, but I find it difficult to trust others completely, or to depend on them. I worry that I will be hurt if I allow myself to become too close to others.

### Style C -- Preoccupied

> I want to be completely emotionally intimate with others, but I often find that others are reluctant to get as close as I would like. I am uncomfortable being without close relationships, but I sometimes worry that others don't value me as much as I value them.

### Style D -- Dismissing

> I am comfortable without close emotional relationships. It is very important to me to feel independent and self-sufficient, and I prefer not to depend on others or have others depend on me.

## Chinese Version (中文版参考译文)

The RQ has been used in Chinese attachment research, typically within studies employing the ECR (Li & Kato, 2006). The four-paragraph descriptions translated for Chinese research contexts are presented below as reference translations:

### 类型A -- 安全型

> 我很容易在情感上与别人亲近。我能安心地依赖他们，也能让他们依赖我。我不担心自己会被孤立，也不担心别人不接受我。

### 类型B -- 恐惧型

> 我不太愿意与别人亲近。我希望建立亲密的情感关系，但我发现很难完全信任别人，也很难依赖他们。我担心如果让自己与别人太过亲近，我会受到伤害。

### 类型C -- 专注型（沉迷型）

> 我想和别人建立完全的情感亲密关系，但我常常发现别人并不愿意像我期望的那样亲近。没有亲密关系让我感到不安，但有时候我担心别人不像我重视他们那样重视我。

### 类型D -- 冷漠型（拒绝型）

> 没有亲密的情感关系我也觉得自在。对我来说，感到独立和自给自足是非常重要的，我不喜欢依赖别人，也不希望别人依赖我。

> **Note:** The above Chinese translations are reference versions compiled from Chinese attachment research literature. For formal research use, consult the validated Chinese adaptations used in specific published studies (e.g., Li & Kato, 2006; Wu & Zhang, 2004).

## Reverse Scoring

**Not applicable.** The RQ does not use reverse scoring. Each paragraph is rated independently on a 7-point scale.

---

## Scoring Method

### Method 1: Categorical (Forced Choice)

The participant selects the single paragraph (A, B, C, or D) that best describes them. This yields a categorical attachment classification:

| Letter | Attachment Style |
|:------:|:----------------|
| A | Secure |
| B | Fearful |
| C | Preoccupied |
| D | Dismissing |

### Method 2: Continuous Ratings

Each of the four paragraphs is rated on a 1--7 scale, producing a continuous profile of attachment tendencies.

### Method 3: Dimensional Scores (Griffin & Bartholomew, 1994)

Two underlying dimensions can be computed from the four continuous ratings:

| Dimension | Formula |
|:----------|:--------|
| **Model of Self** | (Secure + Dismissing) -- (Fearful + Preoccupied) |
| **Model of Other** | (Secure + Preoccupied) -- (Fearful + Dismissing) |

- Higher **Model of Self** scores = more positive self-model (low attachment anxiety).
- Higher **Model of Other** scores = more positive other-model (low attachment avoidance).

## Psychometric Properties

### Original Study (Bartholomew & Horowitz, 1991)

| Property | Value |
|:---------|:------|
| Sample | 77 university students |
| Method | Self-report + friend report + interview |
| Convergent validity | Self-ratings converged with peer ratings and interview classifications |
| Discriminant validity | Four-category model showed better differentiation than three-category model |

### Test-Retest Reliability

| Study | Interval | Stability |
|:------|:---------|:----------|
| Scharfe & Bartholomew (1994) | 8 months | 70% classification stability |
| Klohnen et al. (2005) | Various | Moderate to good temporal stability |

### Structural Validity

- The four attachment prototypes conform to the theoretically predicted 2x2 circumplex structure (Model of Self x Model of Other).
- The continuous rating approach is generally recommended over the forced-choice approach for greater statistical power and sensitivity.
- The RQ shows convergent validity with the longer Relationship Scales Questionnaire (RSQ; Griffin & Bartholomew, 1994) and the Experiences in Close Relationships scale (ECR; Brennan, Clark, & Shaver, 1998).

### Limitations

- Each attachment style is assessed by only a single paragraph item, limiting internal consistency estimation.
- The forced-choice format (Part 1) provides a categorical classification that may not capture the dimensional nature of attachment.
- Continuous ratings (Part 2) are recommended for research use.
