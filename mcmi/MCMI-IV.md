# MCMI-IV — Millon Clinical Multiaxial Inventory, Fourth Edition

## APA Citation

Millon, T., Grossman, S., & Millon, C. (2015). *MCMI-IV: Millon Clinical Multiaxial Inventory manual* (4th ed.). Bloomington, MN: NCS Pearson.

---

## Overview

The MCMI-IV is a **195-item true/false** self-report inventory designed to assess personality disorders and clinical syndromes in **adults currently receiving mental health services**. Grounded in Theodore Millon's evolutionary theory of personality, the MCMI-IV aligns with the DSM-5/DSM-5-TR personality disorder taxonomy and provides three levels of personality functioning interpretation: Normal Style, Abnormal Traits/Type, and Clinical Disorder.

| Property        | Value                     |
|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Total items     | 195                       |
| Response format | True / False              |
| Total scales    | 30 (25 clinical + 5 validity/modifying) |
| Admin time      | 25–35 min                 |
| Reading level   | 5th grade (US)            |
| Age range       | 18+                       |
| Population      | Clinical (patients currently in mental health treatment) |

## Scale Structure

### Modifying Indices / Validity Indicators (5 scales)

| Scale | Abbr | Description |
|-------|:----:|-------------|
| Validity | V | 3 items detecting absurd/random responding |
| Disclosure | X | Openness vs. defensiveness in responding |
| Desirability | Y | Socially desirable self-presentation |

### Grossman Facet Scales (45 facets)

Each of the 15 personality scales (12 Clinical + 3 Severe) has **3 Grossman Facet Scales** (15 x 3 = 45 total), based on Seth Grossman's personality polarities. Example facets for select scales:

| Personality Scale | Facet 1 | Facet 2 | Facet 3 |
|-------------------|---------|---------|---------|
| Schizoid (1)      | Interpersonally Unengaged | Meager Content | Temperamentally Apathetic |
| Avoidant (2A)     | Interpersonally Aversive | Alienated Self-Image | Vexatious Content |
| Melancholic (2B)  | Cognitively Fatalistic | Worthless Self-Image | Temperamentally Woeful |
| Borderline (C)    | Interpersonally Paradoxical | Uncertain Self-Image | Temperamentally Labile |

*(Full listing of all 45 facet scales is in the MCMI-IV manual.)*

## Scoring

- Uses **Base Rate (BR) scores** rather than T-scores, calibrated to clinical base rates of disorders in psychiatric populations.
- **BR ≥ 75:** Presence of clinically significant trait/feature.
- **BR ≥ 85:** Prominence of the disorder; likely meets diagnostic criteria.

---

## Psychometric Properties

- **Internal consistency:** Average Cronbach's alpha = .83 across scales (clinical samples).
- **Test-retest reliability:** Average r = .80 (5–14 day interval in clinical samples); Taiwan study found average r = .68.
- **Convergent validity:** Personality pattern scales correlate with corresponding MMPI-2-RF scales and NEO-PI-R domains.
- **Discriminant validity:** Clinical syndrome scales successfully distinguish patient groups from controls on most scales.
- **Standardization:** Clinical normative sample; BR scores anchored to observed prevalence rates.

## Sample Items (from Pearson sample reports, publicly available)

1. "People have said in the past that I became too interested and too excited about too many things." *(True/False)*
2. "I've had many periods in my life when I was so cheerful and used up so much energy that I fell into a low mood." *(True/False)*
3. "I have always had less interest in sex than most people do." *(True/False)*

*(Items drawn from publicly posted Pearson MCMI-IV sample interpretive reports.)*

## Chinese Version Status

- A **Taiwan Chinese version** has been investigated. A Master's thesis (Kaohsiung Medical University, 2020) examined psychometric properties of the MCMI-IV in Taiwan psychiatric patients and healthy controls. Findings:
  - Average Cronbach's alpha = .83; average test-retest r = .68.
  - Most scales distinguished clinical from control groups, except Antisocial, Alcohol Use, Drug Use, and Paranoid.
  - Turbulent, Anxiety, and PTSD scales had limited validity in the control group.
- **Mainland China:** The MCMI-III (third edition) has been used in research, but a formally published mainland Chinese edition of the MCMI-IV is **not yet available** as of 2025.
