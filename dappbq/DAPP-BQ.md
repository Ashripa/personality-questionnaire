# DAPP-BQ — Dimensional Assessment of Personality Pathology–Basic Questionnaire

## APA Citation

Livesley, W. J., & Jackson, D. N. (2009). *Manual for the Dimensional Assessment of Personality Pathology–Basic Questionnaire*. Port Huron, MI: Sigma Press.

Livesley, W. J., Jang, K. L., & Vernon, P. A. (1998). Phenotypic and genetic structure of traits delineating personality disorder. *Archives of General Psychiatry, 55*(10), 941–948.

## Overview

The DAPP-BQ is a **290-item self-report questionnaire** designed to provide a comprehensive dimensional assessment of personality pathology in clinical populations. Rather than classifying individuals into categorical personality disorder types, it measures the **trait dimensions** underlying personality dysfunction, organized into **18 primary trait scales** and **4 higher-order domains**.

| Property        | Value                     |
|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Total items     | 290                       |
| Primary scales  | 18                        |
| Higher-order domains | 4                    |
| Response format | 5-point Likert            |
| Admin time      | 40–60 min                 |
| Population      | Adults; clinical and non-clinical |

---

## Scale Structure

### 4 Higher-Order Domains with 18 Primary Trait Scales

#### Domain 1: Emotional Dysregulation

| Scale | Items | Description |
|-------|:-----:|-------------|
| Anxiousness | 16 | Pervasive feelings of worry and tension |
| Affective Lability | 16 | Rapid, unpredictable emotional shifts |
| Cognitive Dysregulation | 16 | Thought disorganization, dissociation, depersonalization |
| Identity Problems | 16 | Uncertainty about self-concept, goals, values |
| Insecure Attachment | 16 | Fear of rejection and abandonment in relationships |
| Oppositionality | 16 | Passive resistance, stubbornness, hostile compliance |
| Submissiveness | 16 | Excessive yielding to others, difficulty asserting self |
| Social Avoidance | 16 | Avoidance of social interaction due to anxiety |
| Narcissism | 16 | Grandiosity, entitlement, need for admiration |

#### Domain 2: Inhibitedness (Social Avoidance)

| Scale | Items | Description |
|-------|:-----:|-------------|
| Intimacy Problems | 16 | Difficulty forming and maintaining close relationships |
| Restricted Expression | 16 | Emotional constriction, limited affective display |

*(Social Avoidance also loads on this domain; listed above under Emotional Dysregulation as it cross-loads.)*

#### Domain 3: Dissocial Behavior

| Scale | Items | Description |
|-------|:-----:|-------------|
| Callousness | 16 | Lack of empathy, disregard for others' feelings |
| Conduct Problems | 16 | Rule-breaking, delinquent behavior patterns |
| Stimulus Seeking | 16 | Need for excitement, risk-taking, impulsivity |
| Rejection | 16 | Hostility toward and contempt for others |
| Suspiciousness | 14 | Mistrust of others' motives |
| Self-Harm | 12 | Self-damaging behaviors, suicidal ideation |

#### Domain 4: Compulsivity

| Scale | Items | Description |
|-------|:-----:|-------------|
| Compulsivity | 16 | Rigidity, perfectionism, orderliness |

**Note:** Compulsivity stands as a largely independent domain with its own single scale. Not all primary scales load cleanly onto a single domain; the hierarchical structure has been further investigated at 5-level solutions.

### Item Distribution Summary

- Most scales: **16 items** each
- Suspiciousness: **14 items**
- Self-Harm: **12 items**
- Total: 290 items

---

## Response Format

5-point Likert scale:
1 = *Very unlike me*
2 = *Unlike me*
3 = *Neither like nor unlike me*
4 = *Like me*
5 = *Very like me*

---

## Administration

- **Mode:** Paper-and-pencil; can be administered individually or in groups
- **Population:** Adults aged 18+; designed primarily for clinical/psychiatric populations but also used in non-clinical research
- **Translations:** Available in English, Dutch, German, Italian, Japanese, Norwegian, Spanish, and Chinese (research version)

---

## Psychometric Properties

- **Internal consistency:** Cronbach's alpha = .83–.94 across scales in clinical samples; generally >.80 in non-clinical samples.
- **Test-retest reliability:** r = .81–.93 (3-week interval; Livesley & Jackson, 2009).
- **Factor structure:** Four higher-order factors (Emotional Dysregulation, Inhibitedness, Dissocial Behavior, Compulsivity) consistently replicated across cultures (Canada, Germany, Norway, Japan, China).
- **Five-level hierarchical model:** Goldberg's bass-ackwards analysis reveals a five-level hierarchy that enhances prediction of DSM PD symptoms, particularly Borderline PD.
- **Convergent validity:** Scales correlate strongly with NEO-PI-R personality domains (especially Neuroticism), MMPI-2 clinical scales, and DSM-IV PD diagnostic counts.
- **Behavior genetic studies:** Twin studies show substantial heritability (.40–.60) for most DAPP-BQ scales.

## Chinese Version Status

- **Zheng, W., Wang, W., Huang, Z., Sun, C., Zhu, J., & Livesley, W. J. (2002).** The structure of traits delineating personality disorder in a Chinese sample. *Journal of Personality Disorders, 16*(5), 477–486.
  - This study applied the DAPP-BQ to Chinese participants and found that the four-factor structure (Emotional Dysregulation, Inhibitedness, Dissocial Behavior, Compulsivity) was **replicated** in the Chinese sample.
- The Chinese version is a **research translation** used primarily in academic settings; a commercially published and fully normed Chinese edition is **not widely available** as of 2025.
- Cross-cultural comparisons confirm structural similarity between Chinese and Western samples, supporting the universality of personality pathology dimensions.
