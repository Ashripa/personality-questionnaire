## Key Differences: MMPI-2-RF vs. MMPI-3

- MMPI-3 was re-normed on a 2018-2020 normative sample (N = 1,620), more representative of U.S. demographics.
- MMPI-3 introduces several new Specific Problems scales (e.g., Compulsivity) and reorganizes others.
- MMPI-3 removes outdated items and introduces new content reflecting current clinical constructs.
- Both map onto the HiTOP (Hierarchical Taxonomy of Psychopathology) framework.
- MMPI-3 is the recommended instrument for new implementations.

---

## Scoring

- All scales scored as Uniform T-scores (M = 50, SD = 10).
- Clinical significance threshold: T >= 65 for most scales; T >= 80 for some validity scales.
- Comparison groups available for forensic, medical, and specific clinical populations.

---

## Psychometric Properties

| Property | MMPI-2-RF | MMPI-3 |
|---|---|---|
| Internal Consistency (RC Scales) | .60 -- .89 | .63 -- .90 |
| Test-Retest (1 week) | .55 -- .93 | .60 -- .93 |
| Normative Sample | MMPI-2 restandardization sample | 1,620 new participants (2018--2020) |

- Construct validity supported by extensive correlation with external criteria.
- Hierarchical structure aligns with contemporary models of psychopathology (HiTOP, DSM-5 Section III).

---

## Sample Items

Exact items are copyrighted. The format consists of statements to which examinees respond True or False. General topics cover physical symptoms, emotional states, attitudes, beliefs, and past experiences -- similar in nature to MMPI-2 items (e.g., "I have a good appetite"; "I wake up fresh and rested most mornings").

---

## Chinese Version Status

- **MMPI-2-RF**: No widely published official Chinese adaptation has been documented in the search literature. The Chinese MMPI-2 (CUHK/Institute of Psychology) remains the primary Chinese-language MMPI instrument.
- **MMPI-3**: No Chinese adaptation is currently documented (as of 2026). Given the MMPI-3's 2020 release, adaptation work may be in progress.
- The existing Chinese MMPI-2 norms (Song Weizhen / Cheung Fanny M.) continue to be used in Chinese clinical practice.
