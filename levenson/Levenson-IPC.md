# Levenson IPC: Multidimensional Locus of Control Scales

## Citation

Levenson, H. (1981). Differentiating among internality, powerful others, and chance. In H. M. Lefcourt (Ed.), *Research with the locus of control construct* (Vol. 1, pp. 15-63). New York: Academic Press.

Original scale development:

Levenson, H. (1973). Multidimensional locus of control in psychiatric patients. *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*, *41*(3), 397-404. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0035357

Chinese adaptation:

Lao, R. C. (1978). Levenson's IPC (Internal-External Control) Scale: A comparison of Chinese and American students. *Journal of Cross-Cultural Psychology*, *9*(1), 113-124. https://doi.org/10.1177/002202217891009

## Overview

The Levenson IPC Scales are a 24-item self-report measure that assesses three distinct dimensions of generalized locus of control beliefs:

- **I (Internality)**: The belief that one can control one's own life outcomes through personal effort, ability, and actions.
- **P (Powerful Others)**: The belief that powerful other people (authority figures, institutions, pressure groups) control one's life outcomes.
- **C (Chance)**: The belief that life outcomes are determined by fate, luck, or chance.

This multidimensional approach was developed to address limitations of Rotter's (1966) unidimensional Internal-External scale, by separating externality into two conceptually distinct components (Powerful Others vs. Chance).

## Reverse Scoring

There are **no reverse-scored items**. All items are scored in the direction they are worded.

## Scoring Method

1. For each subscale, sum the responses to its 8 items (raw sum ranges from -24 to +24).
2. Add a **constant of 24** to each subscale sum to eliminate negative values.
3. Each respondent receives **three independent scores** (I, P, C), each ranging from **0 to 48**.
4. Higher scores indicate stronger endorsement of that locus of control orientation.
5. The three subscales are treated as **independent dimensions**, not combined into a single score. A person can score high on more than one dimension.

### Score Interpretation

| Score Range | Interpretation |
|-------------|----------------|
| 0--16 | Low endorsement |
| 17--31 | Moderate endorsement |
| 32--48 | High endorsement |

## Chinese Version

A Chinese version of the Levenson IPC Scales was developed and validated by Lao (1978) for use with Chinese (Taiwanese) college students. Key findings:

- The three-factor structure (I, P, C) was replicated in Chinese samples.
- Similar patterns of gender differences were found for both American and Chinese students on the IPC dimensions.
- The Internal, Powerful Others, and Chance dimensions correlated with a similar number of confidence and expectancy variables across both cultures, suggesting that the locus of control variable taps a basic psychological dimension common to both cultures.

**Note**: The specific Chinese item translations were not publicly available from the sources consulted. Researchers wishing to use the Chinese version should contact the scale authors or consult the Lao (1978) publication and subsequent Chinese adaptation studies.

## Psychometric Properties

### Original Validation (Levenson, 1973, 1981)

| Property | I | P | C |
|----------|---|---|---|
| Cronbach's alpha | 0.64 | 0.77 | 0.78 |
| 7-week test-retest *r* | 0.64 | 0.74 | 0.78 |

- Factor analysis supported the three-factor structure distinguishing I, P, and C as separate dimensions.
- The P and C subscales tend to correlate moderately with each other (both are external orientations), while both show low or negative correlations with the I subscale.

### Greek Validation (Kourmousi et al., 2015; N = 3,668)

| Property | I | P | C |
|----------|---|---|---|
| Cronbach's alpha | 0.65 | 0.76 | 0.73 |

- The three-factor structure was confirmed through confirmatory factor analysis.
- Measurement invariance was supported across gender.

### Cross-Cultural (Lao, 1978; Chinese and American students)

- Structural equivalence of the IPC across Chinese and American samples was supported.
- Chinese students scored significantly higher on the P (Powerful Others) subscale compared to American students, consistent with cultural differences in power distance.

### Content Design Notes

The 24 items are organized around **four life domains**, with parallel items across the three subscales:

| Domain | I Item | P Item | C Item |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Leadership | 1 | 8 | 16 |
| Car accidents | 4 | 20 | 12 |
| Plans/goals | 5 | 22 | 14 |
| Friends | 9 | 17 | 24 |
| General life control | 18, 23 | 3, 11 | 2, 10 |
| Personal interests | 19 | 13 | 6 |
| Getting what one wants | 21 | 15 | 7 |
