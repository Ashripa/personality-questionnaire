## Scale Structure

### Two-Factor / Four-Facet Model

**Factor 1: Interpersonal/Affective** (8 items, max = 16)

| Facet | Items | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Facet 1: Interpersonal | Items 1, 2, 4, 5 | Social presentation style: charm, grandiosity, lying, manipulation |
| Facet 2: Affective | Items 6, 7, 8, 16 | Emotional deficits: lack of remorse, shallow affect, no empathy, failure to accept responsibility |

**Factor 2: Lifestyle/Antisocial** (10 items, max = 20)

| Facet | Items | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Facet 3: Lifestyle | Items 3, 9, 13, 14, 15 | Impulsive, irresponsible, parasitic, lacking goals, stimulation-seeking |
| Facet 4: Antisocial | Items 10, 12, 18, 19, 20 | Behavioral problems, delinquency, revocation, criminal versatility |

**Items not loading on either factor**: Items 11 (Promiscuous Sexual Behavior) and 17 (Many Short-Term Marital Relationships).

## Scoring and Interpretation

| Score Range | Classification |
|---|---|
| 0--19 | No psychopathy |
| 20--29 | Possible psychopathic traits |
| 30--40 | Psychopathy (research/diagnostic threshold) |

- The conventional diagnostic cutoff is **30** (out of 40) in North American forensic research.
- European research sometimes uses lower thresholds (e.g., 25).
- Scores are dimensional; higher scores indicate more psychopathic traits.
- Prorated scoring is used if 5 or fewer items are omitted.

---

## Administration Requirements

- **Trained rater**: Must be a licensed mental health professional with specialized PCL-R training.
- **Interview**: A semi-structured interview lasting 90--120 minutes covers life history, relationships, employment, antisocial behavior, and affect.
- **Collateral information**: File review (criminal records, institutional reports, prior assessments) is essential; the PCL-R should never be scored based on interview alone.
- **MHS offers certified training workshops** for proper administration and scoring.

## Psychometric Properties

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Internal Consistency (alpha) | .85 -- .87 |
| Interrater Reliability (ICC, single rater) | .78 -- .94 (M = .86) |
| Interrater Reliability (ICC, average of two raters) | .87 -- .97 (M = .93) |
| Test-Retest | Generally high across intervals |
| Standard Error of Measurement | ~3 points |

- **Predictive validity**: Robust prediction of recidivism, institutional violence, and treatment non-compliance.
- **Construct validity**: Factor structure replicated across samples (North American, European, forensic psychiatric).
- **Field reliability concern**: Research-setting ICC values are higher than real-world forensic evaluation contexts, where adversarial conditions may reduce agreement.

## Sample Items (illustrative descriptions -- the PCL-R items are trait descriptions, not self-report questions)

The PCL-R is not a self-report questionnaire. Raters score each item based on interview and file data. Publicly available item descriptions include:

1. **Glibness / Superficial Charm**: "A smooth, engaging, charming conversational style that appears rehearsed rather than genuine."
2. **Grandiose Sense of Self-Worth**: "An inflated view of one's abilities, importance, or status, disproportionate to actual achievements."
3. **Shallow Affect**: "Emotional reactions that appear flat, short-lived, or performed rather than genuinely felt."

> Note: These are paraphrased trait descriptions from published educational and review sources. The actual scoring criteria are detailed in the copyrighted PCL-R manual.

## Chinese Version Status

- No widely documented standardized Chinese version of the PCL-R exists in the mainstream English-language literature as of 2026.
- The PCL-R was developed primarily on Western (North American) male forensic populations, and cross-cultural concerns have been raised about its applicability in non-Western settings.
- Chinese forensic psychology research has used translated versions of psychopathy measures, but a formally published and normed Chinese PCL-R with local normative data has not been prominently documented.
- Related instruments: The Psychopathic Personality Inventory (PPI) and the Levenson Self-Report Psychopathy Scale (LSRP) have been adapted for Chinese populations in research contexts.
