## Construct

The HSPS measures **Sensory Processing Sensitivity (SPS)**, a temperament trait characterized by deeper cognitive processing of stimuli, greater emotional reactivity, heightened awareness of environmental subtleties, and susceptibility to overstimulation. Approximately 15--20% of the population scores high on SPS.

### Factor Structure

The original scale was designed as a **unidimensional** measure. Smolewska et al. (2006) later identified a three-factor structure through exploratory factor analysis:

| Factor | Abbreviation | Description |
|--------|:------------:|-------------|
| Ease of Excitation | EOE | Being easily overwhelmed by internal and external demands |
| Low Sensory Threshold | LST | Unpleasant sensory arousal from external stimuli |
| Aesthetic Sensitivity | AES | Awareness of and pleasure in aesthetics, subtleties, and inner life |

The Chinese version (Zhang & Zhang, 2023) identified a **six-factor** structure:

| Chinese Factor | English Translation |
|----------------|---------------------|
| 情绪反应 | Emotional Reactivity |
| 低感觉阈限 | Low Sensory Threshold |
| 易兴奋性 | Ease of Excitation |
| 审美敏感性 | Aesthetic Sensitivity |
| 惩罚敏感性 | Punishment Sensitivity |
| 加工深度 | Depth of Processing |

---

## Reverse Scoring

**No items are reverse-scored.** All items are positively keyed (higher ratings = greater sensitivity).

---

## Scoring

### Total Score

**Total SPS Score** = Sum of all 27 items (range 27--189).

- A commonly cited clinical cutoff is a **mean score of 4.0 or above** (i.e., total score >= 108) to indicate high sensitivity, though this is a rough guideline rather than a validated diagnostic threshold.
- Some self-report versions use a simpler "true/false" format where answering "true" to 14 or more items suggests high sensitivity.

### Factor Scores (Smolewska et al., 2006)

| Factor | Items | Computation |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| EOE (Ease of Excitation) | 3, 4, 6, 13, 14, 16, 17, 20, 21, 23, 26, 27 | Mean or sum |
| LST (Low Sensory Threshold) | 1, 7, 9, 11, 18, 19, 24, 25 | Mean or sum |
| AES (Aesthetic Sensitivity) | 2, 5, 8, 10, 12, 15, 22 | Mean or sum |

### Factor Analysis

- **Unidimensional**: The original scale was designed and validated as a single-factor measure (Aron & Aron, 1997).
- **Three-factor model** (Smolewska et al., 2006): EOE, LST, AES. This structure has been widely replicated across cultures.
- **Six-factor model** (Chinese version; Zhang & Zhang, 2023): Emotional Reactivity, Low Sensory Threshold, Ease of Excitation, Aesthetic Sensitivity, Punishment Sensitivity, Depth of Processing.

### Convergent/Discriminant Validity

- SPS correlates moderately with **introversion** (*r* ~ .30--.40) but is a distinct construct.
- SPS correlates moderately with **neuroticism** (*r* ~ .40--.50), particularly for EOE and LST factors; AES shows weaker or no correlation with neuroticism.
- SPS is distinct from clinical conditions such as ASD, PTSD, and sensory processing disorder.
- AES correlates positively with **openness to experience**.
- EOE correlates with **Behavioral Inhibition System (BIS)** activation.
