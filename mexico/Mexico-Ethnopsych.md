# Mexican Ethnopsychology Inventory

**Priority:** P5
**Category:** Indigenous / culturally-derived personality assessment
**Status:** Research instrument (primarily academic use in Mexico and Latin America)

---

## Overview

Mexican Ethnopsychology is a research paradigm and associated set of personality inventories developed by Rogelio Diaz-Guerrero and colleagues, designed to measure personality constructs that are meaningful and indigenous to Mexican culture. The approach explicitly rejects the assumption that personality constructs developed in Anglo-Saxon contexts are universal, instead deriving constructs empirically from Mexican cultural premises.

## Theoretical Foundation

### The Historico-Bio-Psycho-Socio-Cultural (HBPSC) Framework

Diaz-Guerrero proposed that human behavior must be understood through the integration of:
- **Historical** factors: The specific history of a people
- **Biological** factors: Innate human characteristics
- **Psychological** factors: Individual cognitive and emotional processes
- **Social** factors: Group dynamics and interpersonal relationships
- **Cultural** factors: Shared beliefs, norms, values, and traditions

### Premisas Historico-Socio-Culturales (PHSCs)
- Core concept: culturally shared beliefs and norms that guide behavior
- Examples: "A good wife should always be faithful to her husband"; "Children should always obey their parents"
- These premises are empirically measurable and vary across cultures
- They function as the interface between culture and individual personality

## Key Constructs and Scales

### 1. Filosofia de Vida (Philosophy of Life)
- Measures cultural value orientations
- Includes dimensions like affiliative obedience vs. active self-assertion
- Captures the Mexican emphasis on interpersonal harmony and family loyalty

### 2. Autoconcepto (Self-Concept)
- Ethnopsychological self-concept inventory
- Dimensions derived from Mexican semantic differential studies
- Includes culturally specific facets not captured by Western self-concept measures

### 3. Premisas Historico-Socio-Culturales (PHSCs) Scale
- Measures endorsement of culturally shared beliefs
- Includes subscales for:
  - Machismo
  - Abnegation/self-sacrifice (especially female roles)
  - Virginity/honor
  - Family honor and obedience
  - Respect for authority
  - Cultural rigidity vs. flexibility

### 4. Locus of Control (Mexican adaptation)
- Adapted to include culturally relevant dimensions
- Distinguishes between internal, external-powerful others, and external-fate attributions

## Methodology

### Scale Development Process
1. Begin with open-ended exploration of culturally meaningful concepts
2. Use semantic differential and associative techniques with Mexican samples
3. Derive constructs empirically from within the culture (emic approach)
4. Validate through factor analysis and cross-cultural comparison
5. Avoid translating foreign instruments; build indigenous measures from the ground up

### Research Approach
- Combines quantitative psychometrics with cultural anthropology
- Uses cross-cultural comparison (especially Mexico vs. United States) as a validation strategy
- Emphasizes ecological validity within Mexican culture

## Psychometric Properties

### Reliability
- Internal consistency generally adequate (alpha .70-.85 for major scales)
- Varies across subscales and versions

### Validity
- Content validity: Established through extensive qualitative and ethnographic work
- Construct validity: Factor structures replicated across Mexican samples
- Cross-cultural validity: Meaningful differences found between Mexican and U.S. samples
- Criterion validity: PHSCs predict culturally relevant behaviors (family dynamics, gender roles, coping styles)

### Limitations
- Most instruments published only in Spanish
- Norms primarily based on Mexican urban and university samples
- Some constructs may reflect historical Mexican culture more than contemporary values
- Limited adoption outside Mexico and Latin American psychology
- Some scales reflect gender role assumptions that have evolved since original development
- Scoring systems not always fully standardized

## Impact and Legacy

- Diaz-Guerrero is considered the founder of Mexican ethnopsychology and one of the most influential psychologists in Latin American history
- The approach inspired indigenous psychology movements in other countries
- Contributed to the critique of cultural imperialism in psychology
- Demonstrated that personality constructs are not universally exportable
- Influenced the cross-cultural psychology movement globally

## Access

- **Published:** Primarily through Mexican academic publishers (Trillas, Manual Moderno)
- **Language:** Spanish (some scales have English research versions)
- **Cost:** Available through Mexican psychology publishers
- **Availability:** Some scales published in journal articles; others in book-length manuals
- **Training:** Familiarity with Mexican culture and Spanish language essential for valid administration and interpretation

## Notes

- Mexican Ethnopsychology represents one of the most developed indigenous psychology programs worldwide
- It predates and influenced the broader indigenous psychology movement that emerged in Asia in the 1990s
- The HBPSC framework remains a valuable model for culturally grounded personality research
- Contemporary Mexican psychologists continue to update and extend Diaz-Guerrero's work, particularly regarding changing gender norms and urbanization effects
