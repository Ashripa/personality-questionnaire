# Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale (5DC) & Curiosity and Exploration Inventory-II (CEI-II)

This file covers two curiosity measures by Todd Kashdan and colleagues.

---

## Part 1: Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale (5DC)

### APA Citation

Kashdan, T. B., Stiksma, M. C., Disabato, D. J., McKnight, P. E., Bekier, J., Kaji, J., & Lazarus, R. (2018). The five-dimensional curiosity scale: Capturing the bandwidth of curiosity and identifying four unique subgroups of curious people. *Journal of Research in Personality, 73*, 130--149. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jrp.2017.11.011

### Revised version (5DCR)

Kashdan, T. B., Disabato, D. J., Goodman, F. R., & McKnight, P. E. (2020). The Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale Revised (5DCR): Briefer subscales while separating overt and covert social curiosity. *Personality and Individual Differences, 157*, 109836. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2020.109836

### Overview

The 5DC is a 25-item self-report measure assessing five dimensions of curiosity. Respondents rate how well each statement generally describes them. The scale was developed across multiple studies (N > 3,000 in the confirmatory sample).

### Instructions (English)

> Below are statements people often use to describe themselves. Please read each statement and indicate **how accurately each statement describes you in general** using the following scale.

### Response Scale

**1** = *Does Not Describe Me At All* ... **7** = *Completely Describes Me*

### All 25 Items by Subscale

#### Subscale 1: Joyous Exploration (JE)

| # | Item |
|--:|:--|
| 1 | I view challenging situations as an opportunity to grow and learn. |
| 2 | I am always looking for experiences that challenge how I think about myself and the world. |
| 3 | I seek out situations where it is likely that I will have to think in depth about something. |
| 4 | I enjoy learning about subjects that are unfamiliar to me. |
| 5 | I find it fascinating to learn new information. |

#### Subscale 2: Deprivation Sensitivity (DS)

| # | Item |
|--:|:--|
| 6 | Thinking about solutions to difficult conceptual problems can keep me awake at night. |
| 7 | I can spend hours on a single problem because I just can't rest without knowing the answer. |
| 8 | I feel frustrated if I can't figure out the solution to a problem, so I work even harder to solve it. |
| 9 | I work relentlessly at problems that I feel must be solved. |
| 10 | It bothers me if I don't know the answer to a question so I search for the answer. |

#### Subscale 3: Stress Tolerance (ST)

| # | Item |
|--:|:--|
| 11 | The anxiety of not knowing the answer to a question is hard for me to handle. (R) |
| 12 | I cannot handle the stress that comes from entering uncertain situations. (R) |
| 13 | I find it hard to explore new places when I lack confidence in my abilities. (R) |
| 14 | It is difficult to concentrate when there is a possibility that I will be taken by surprise. (R) |
| 15 | Risk-taking is exciting to me. |

#### Subscale 4: Social Curiosity (SC)

| # | Item |
|--:|:--|
| 16 | I like finding out why people behave the way they do. |
| 17 | I like to learn about the habits of others. |
| 18 | When other people are having a conversation, I like to find out what it's about. |
| 19 | When around other people, I like listening to their conversations. |
| 20 | I seek out information about the personal lives of others. |

#### Subscale 5: Thrill Seeking (TS)

| # | Item |
|--:|:--|
| 21 | The anxious feeling of risk is exciting for me. |
| 22 | I prefer friends who are excitingly unpredictable. |
| 23 | I like to do things that are a little frightening. |
| 24 | I would like to explore a strange city or section of town, even if it means getting lost. |
| 25 | I prefer to take the adventurous route even if I am not sure where it will lead. |

### Reverse-Scored Items

Items **11, 12, 13, 14** (Stress Tolerance subscale, marked with "(R)") are reverse-scored.

Reverse scoring: 1 -> 7, 2 -> 6, 3 -> 5, 4 -> 4, 5 -> 3, 6 -> 2, 7 -> 1

> **Note on Stress Tolerance:** Items 11--14 are negatively worded (low stress tolerance = high curiosity barrier). After reverse-scoring, higher scores indicate **greater** stress tolerance (i.e., the person is more comfortable with the anxiety of the unknown). Item 15 ("Risk-taking is exciting to me") is scored in the positive direction and does NOT need to be reversed.

### Scoring Method

1. Reverse-score items 11--14.
2. Compute the **mean** (or sum) of the 5 items within each subscale.
3. Subscale score range: 5--35 (sum) or 1.0--7.0 (mean).
4. Total curiosity score = sum of all 25 items (range: 25--175) or mean of all 25 items.
5. Higher scores indicate higher curiosity on each dimension.

### Psychometric Properties

| Property | Values |
|:--|:--|
| Internal consistency (alpha) | JE: .86; DS: .83; ST: .79; SC: .88; TS: .84 (Study 3, N = 3,000) |
| Test-retest reliability (4 months) | .72 -- .82 across subscales |
| Factor structure | 5-factor CFA supported; CFI = .93, RMSEA = .06 |
| Convergent validity | JE correlates with openness, DS with need for cognition, SC with interpersonal curiosity |
| Discriminant validity | Five factors are intercorrelated but distinct (.10 -- .45 range) |

## Part 2: Curiosity and Exploration Inventory-II (CEI-II)

### APA Citation

Kashdan, T. B., Gallagher, M. W., Silvia, P. J., Winterstein, B. P., Breen, W. E., Terhar, D., & Steger, M. F. (2009). The Curiosity and Exploration Inventory-II (CEI-II): Development, factor structure, and psychometrics. *Journal of Research in Personality, 43*(6), 987--998. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jrp.2009.04.011

### Overview

The CEI-II is a 10-item self-report scale measuring dispositional curiosity. It captures two facets: **Stretching** (the motivation to seek out knowledge and new experiences) and **Embracing** (the willingness to embrace the novel, uncertain, and unpredictable nature of everyday life).

### Instructions (English)

> Below are statements that many people would find desirable, but we want you to answer only in terms of what you truly believe or experience. Please read each statement and indicate how accurately each statement describes you using the following scale:

### Response Scale

**1** = *Very Slightly or Not At All*; **2** = *A Little*; **3** = *Moderately*; **4** = *Quite a Bit*; **5** = *Extremely*

### All 10 Items

| # | Subscale | Item (English) |
|--:|:--|:--|
| 1 | Stretching | I actively seek as much information as I can in new situations. |
| 2 | Embracing | I am the type of person who really enjoys the uncertainty of everyday life. |
| 3 | Stretching | I am at my best when doing something that is complex or challenging. |
| 4 | Embracing | Everywhere I go, I am out looking for new things or experiences. |
| 5 | Stretching | I view challenging situations as an opportunity to grow and learn. |
| 6 | Embracing | I like to do things that are a little frightening. |
| 7 | Stretching | I am always looking for experiences that challenge how I think about myself and the world. |
| 8 | Embracing | I prefer jobs that are excitingly unpredictable. |
| 9 | Stretching | I frequently seek out opportunities to challenge myself and grow as a person. |
| 10 | Embracing | I am the kind of person who embraces unfamiliar people, events, and places. |

### Reverse-Scored Items

**None.** All items are scored in the positive direction.

### Scoring Method

1. **Stretching subscale**: Mean (or sum) of items 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. Range: 5--25 (sum) or 1.0--5.0 (mean).
2. **Embracing subscale**: Mean (or sum) of items 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Range: 5--25 (sum) or 1.0--5.0 (mean).
3. **Total CEI-II score**: Sum of all 10 items (range: 10--50) or mean of all 10 items.
4. Higher scores = greater dispositional curiosity.

### Psychometric Properties

| Property | Values |
|:--|:--|
| Internal consistency (alpha) | Total: .86; Stretching: .78; Embracing: .75 |
| Test-retest reliability (3 weeks) | r = .72 (total), r = .67 (Stretching), r = .73 (Embracing) |
| Factor structure | 2-factor CFA; CFI = .97, RMSEA = .05 |
| Convergent validity | Correlates with openness to experience (.53), positive affect (.43), well-being (.41) |
| Discriminant validity | Low correlations with neuroticism (-.26) and negative affect (-.15) |
| Mean (college sample) | M = 33.2, SD = 6.5 (total score, 10--50 scale) |
