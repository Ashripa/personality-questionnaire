# DISC Assessment

**Priority:** P5
**Category:** Behavioral style / personality assessment
**Status:** Commercial (multiple vendors)

---

## Overview

DISC is a behavioral assessment tool based on the theory of psychologist William Moulton Marston, which measures four primary behavioral dimensions: Dominance, Influence, Steadiness, and Compliance (or Conscientiousness). Unlike a single standardized instrument, "DISC" refers to a family of assessments produced by many vendors, all based on Marston's original theory.

## Key Citations

- Marston, W. M. (1928). *Emotions of Normal People*. Kegan Paul, Trench, Trubner & Co.
- Wiley (2013). *Everything DiSC Manual*. Wiley.

## Structure

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Items | 24-80 items (varies by vendor/version) |
| Scales | 4 primary dimensions |
| Format | Forced-choice, Likert, or adjective checklist (varies) |
| Administration time | 10-25 minutes |
| Age range | Adults (some youth versions exist) |
| Versions | Everything DiSC (Wiley), DiSC Classic, TTI DISC, Extended DISC, Thomas PPA, etc. |

## Four Dimensions

| Dimension | Description | Behavioral Tendencies |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **D - Dominance** | How you respond to problems and challenges | Direct, results-oriented, firm, strong-willed, forceful |
| **I - Influence** | How you influence others through persuasion | Outgoing, enthusiastic, optimistic, collaborative, lively |
| **S - Steadiness** | How you respond to pace and consistency | Even-tempered, accommodating, patient, humble, tactful |
| **C - Compliance** | How you respond to procedures and constraints | Analytical, reserved, precise, private, systematic |

## Response Format

Varies by vendor:
- **Forced-choice:** Select "most like me" and "least like me" from groups of four adjectives
- **Likert scale:** Rate agreement with behavioral statements
- **Adjective checklist:** Check all adjectives that apply

Most versions produce an ipsative (relative) profile.

## Scoring and Profiles

- Produces a profile showing relative emphasis on each of the four dimensions
- Many versions generate a primary style (D, I, S, or C) and secondary style
- Some versions identify 12-15 substyles (e.g., Di, iD, Si, etc.)
- Results typically presented as a circular or quadrant model

## Psychometric Properties

### Reliability
- Internal consistency: Cronbach's alpha .70-.85 (varies by vendor and version)
- Test-retest reliability: Generally adequate for team development (r = .80-.92 reported for Everything DiSC)

### Validity
- Construct validity: Structural equation modeling supports a four-factor oblique model consistent with Marston's theory
- Convergent validity: Moderate correlations with Big Five (D correlates with Extraversion and low Agreeableness; I with Extraversion; S with Agreeableness; C with Conscientiousness)
- **Predictive validity for job performance: Limited and not well-documented in peer-reviewed literature**
- Face validity: Generally high; users find results recognizable

### Limitations
- Multiple competing vendors with different item sets, norms, and scoring algorithms
- Limited independent peer-reviewed research compared to Big Five instruments
- Ipsative scoring in most versions limits statistical analysis
- Not recommended for employee selection or clinical diagnosis
- Predictive validity for job outcomes is weak
- Theory predates modern psychometric standards

## Theoretical Background

William Moulton Marston (also the creator of Wonder Woman and the lie detector) proposed in 1928 that normal human emotions could be understood along two axes: active vs. passive, and antagonistic vs. favorable environment. The four quadrants produce the DISC types. Marston never created an assessment instrument; others developed tests based on his theory starting in the 1950s-1970s.

## Access

- **Commercial:** Multiple vendors (Wiley/Everything DiSC, TTI Success Insights, Extended DISC, Thomas International)
- **Cost:** $30-150+ per administration depending on vendor and report type
- **Not open-access:** Each vendor's items are proprietary
- **Certification:** Many vendors offer or require practitioner certification

## Notes

- DISC occupies an intermediate scientific position: more commercially validated than the Enneagram, less rigorously studied than the Big Five
- Best used as a communication and team development tool, not for selection or diagnosis
- The strongest scientific case for DISC is as a communication style instrument, not a psychometric personality measure
- Very widely used in corporate training and coaching worldwide
