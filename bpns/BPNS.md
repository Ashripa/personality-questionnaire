# BPNS - Basic Psychological Needs Scale

## Overview

The Basic Psychological Needs Scale (BPNS) is a self-report measure grounded in Self-Determination Theory (SDT; Deci & Ryan, 2000). It assesses the degree to which an individual's three fundamental psychological needs -- Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness -- are satisfied. These three needs are theorized to be essential for optimal functioning, growth, and well-being.

---

## Citation

- **Theoretical framework:** Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (2000). The "what" and "why" of goal pursuits: Human needs and the self-determination of behavior. *Psychological Inquiry, 11*(4), 227-268. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327965PLI1104_01
- **Scale development:** Gagné, M. (2003). The role of autonomy support and autonomy orientation in prosocial behavior engagement. *Motivation and Emotion, 27*(3), 199-223.
- **Work version:** Deci, E. L., Ryan, R. M., Gagné, M., Leone, D. R., Usunov, J., & Kornazheva, B. P. (2001). Need satisfaction, motivation, and well-being in the work organizations of a former Eastern Bloc country. *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 27*(8), 930-942.
- **Chinese version:** Liu, J. S. (刘俊升) et al. adapted and validated the Chinese version of the BPNS. The Chinese version retains the three-dimensional structure (Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness) with 19 items (two items removed due to poor fit in the Chinese context).

---

## Three Basic Psychological Needs

| Need | Definition | No. of Items |
|------|------------|:------------:|
| **Autonomy** | Feeling volitional and self-endorsed in one's actions; experiencing a sense of choice and psychological freedom | 7 |
| **Competence** | Feeling effective in one's interactions with the environment; experiencing opportunities to express and exercise one's capacities | 6 |
| **Relatedness** | Feeling connected to and cared for by others; experiencing a sense of belonging and warmth in social interactions | 8 |

Please read each of the following items carefully, thinking about how it relates to your life, and then indicate how true it is for you. Use the following scale:

## Scoring

### Step 1: Reverse Score

For items marked "R" in the Scoring column (items 3, 4, 7, 11, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20), subtract the respondent's answer from 8:

```
Reversed score = 8 - original score
```

| Original | Reversed |
|:--------:|:--------:|
| 1 | 7 |
| 2 | 6 |
| 3 | 5 |
| 4 | 4 |
| 5 | 3 |
| 6 | 2 |
| 7 | 1 |

### Step 2: Compute Subscale Scores

Calculate the **mean** (average) of the items within each subscale after reverse scoring:

| Subscale | Items | Score Range |
|----------|-------|:----------:|
| Autonomy | 1, 4(R), 8, 11(R), 14, 17, 20(R) | 1-7 |
| Competence | 3(R), 5, 10, 13, 15(R), 19(R) | 1-7 |
| Relatedness | 2, 6, 7(R), 9, 12, 16(R), 18(R), 21 | 1-7 |

### Step 3: Interpretation

- Higher mean scores indicate greater satisfaction of the corresponding psychological need.
- A total need satisfaction score can be computed by averaging all 21 items (after reverse scoring), though subscale scores are generally more informative.

## Other Versions

The BPNS exists in several domain-specific versions:

| Version | Items | Domain | Reference |
|---------|:-----:|--------|-----------|
| BPNS - General | 21 | General life | Deci & Ryan (2000) |
| BPNS - Work (BPNS-W) | 21 | Work/workplace | Deci et al. (2001) |
| BPNS - Relationships | 9 | Interpersonal relationships | La Guardia et al. (2000) |
| BPNSFS (Satisfaction & Frustration) | 24 | General (measures both satisfaction and frustration) | Chen et al. (2015) |

**Note:** The newer BPNSFS (Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction and Frustration Scale; Chen et al., 2015) is increasingly recommended over the original BPNS as it measures both need satisfaction and need frustration separately, providing a more comprehensive assessment.

## Chinese Version Notes

The Chinese version of the BPNS was adapted and validated by Liu Junsheng (刘俊升) and colleagues. Key findings:

- The Chinese version retains the three-factor structure (Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness).
- Two items were removed from the original 21-item scale due to poor psychometric properties in the Chinese context, resulting in a 19-item Chinese version.
- Each item is rated on the same 1 (completely disagree / 完全不符合) to 7 (completely agree / 非常符合) scale.
- The three subscales demonstrate acceptable reliability and validity in Chinese samples.

Researchers using the Chinese version should consult the original validation study and contact the Center for Self-Determination Theory for permission.

## Psychometric Properties

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Internal consistency (Autonomy) | Cronbach's alpha = 0.69-0.78 |
| Internal consistency (Competence) | Cronbach's alpha = 0.71-0.80 |
| Internal consistency (Relatedness) | Cronbach's alpha = 0.76-0.86 |
| Factor structure | Three correlated factors |
| Convergent validity | Positively correlated with intrinsic motivation, well-being, vitality |
| Discriminant validity | Negatively correlated with ill-being, controlled motivation |
